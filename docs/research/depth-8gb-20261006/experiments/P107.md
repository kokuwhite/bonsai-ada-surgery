## Question

Why is sparse decode slower than dense at 245k (P106: 4.9 vs 5.7 tok/s) but 1.9x dense at 160k? Which op's time
grows with the host tail (1,753 pages at 160k, 3,077 at 245k)?

## Why

P106: with 028f606fc, 160k sparse 18.5 vs dense 9.7; 245k sparse 4.9 vs dense 5.7. At 160k FLASH_ATTN_EXT was 28.5 of
57.8 ms per graph (49 %). docs/DEPTH.md open item (1): "at 245k sparse is still below dense: something in the sparse
decode path grows with the host tail (~3,077 pages at 245k against ~1,753 at 160k)".

## Plan and gate

Notebook (P106 result): "P107 (queue 69e, before P104): OP_TIMING dense vs sparse at 245k -> which op grows with the
host tail." Script header: "per-op timing at 245k, dense vs sparse (028f606fc, bin-sparse6), lookup off, f16 mask,
graphs off. Why is sparse slower than dense at 245k (4.9 vs 5.7) but 1.9x at 160k? Which op grows with the host
tail?" No numeric gate (diagnosis).

## Method

run_arm.sh per arm with quick_tps.py at 245000 (cold ~244k code prompt, then prose and bash 512-token follow-ups,
400-token greedy decodes) with GGML_CUDA_OP_TIMING=1. From each server log, the last "op timing over N graphs" block
(ms per graph per op, share, op count, shape) is saved (first 30 lines); the summary prints the mean decode and the
top 14 op lines per arm. Compare per-op ms per graph dense vs sparse (and against P106 arm a at 160k).

## Deviations, incidents, void runs

- Order: planned "before P104"; the queue ran P104 first (P104 10:06-10:32, P107 from 10:32:56).
- OP_TIMING itself slows decode (P106 arm a vs arm c), so absolute tok/s here are not comparable with P102 / P106

## Verdict

Diagnosis answered, not the expected one. No op grows with the page count. Sparse attention works at 245k
(FLASH_ATTN_EXT 91.8 -> 61.5 ms per graph). The loss is in ops that sparse does not change: the ternary mat-vecs do the
same work in both arms but run 2.5-24x slower in the sparse arm, from the moment the VRAM reading wraps. Reading (not
proven): an allocation at the first sparse decode step takes the card over its total, and WDDM demotes part of the
weights to system memory; the follow-up prefill drop (78 -> 44) is likely the same effect. Test: P109 (margin +200 MiB).

## Corrections later

- P111 (2026-10-13) confirmed the reading. The 4070 measured the sparse-only allocations of 028f606fc at the first
  sparse op: page-bound tables 393 MiB and a whole-tail first fill 385 MiB at 245k (up to ~780 MiB over dense). With
  the 8d5b862fc path and margin 475 in both arms, the sparse arm kept 106 MiB free, MUL_MAT 17408x5120 ran at 0.89x
  dense, and 245k sparse decode was 11.6 vs dense 5.2 tok/s (OP_TIMING on).
- Load value: the sparse arm loaded with 318 MiB free (run_arm "loaded" line, 11:07:51). The 216 MiB at 11:08:55 in
  the Results is the first trace sample, taken after the prefill had started.

## Toolbox (for the next model)

- Method: when a sparse path wins at mid depth and loses at max depth, time every op at max depth in both arms and
  look for the op whose cost scales with the number of pages (selection, bounds, mask build) rather than with the
  kept rows.
- Method that found the real cause here: trace free VRAM every 30 s during each arm (not only at the end), and read
  wrapped `nvidia-smi` values (free near 4,194,304 MiB) as an over-commit. On WDDM an over-commit does not fail an
  allocation; it shows up as unrelated ops (here the weight mat-vecs) slowing down.
- Dense ROPE x8 = 26.3 ms per graph at 245k with per-op sync timing is most likely the tier-copy wait charged to the
  op after it; per-op timing shares are not costs of the op itself.
