## Question

At one column (plain decode), what share of the step is PTQ1_0 mat-vec, at what fraction of DRAM peak, and what share
is GDN / FA / small ops? Which kernel is the lever toward 60 tok/s?

## Why

P35 interim: draft 2 gives code +4.5 %, prose -9 %, acceptance 0.80 -> 0.67. "60 tok/s raw needs the target step
itself faster."

## Plan and gate (as frozen before the result)

"bin-rc2, no head, N=53,248, GGML_CUDA_OP_TIMING=1 (graphs off inside the timer, so absolute ms is inflated; shares
are the data), depths 4k and 32k, LLAMA_ARG_LOG_VERBOSITY=5. Read: per-op share at cols=1; achieved GB/s of each
PTQ1_0 mat-vec vs 448 GB/s peak. Decides which kernel to attack: if PTQ1_0 mat-vecs are > 60% and below 50% of peak
bandwidth, the Turing mat-vec is the lever; if GDN / FA / small ops dominate, fusion is."

## Method

Long mode, quick_tps at 4k and 32k with the op timer on. Mat-vec microbench: test-backend-ops perf, n=1, per weight
shape. Ceiling arithmetic from the weight bytes per token.

## Results

P36 (graphs on): quick_tps decode 43.3 (4k) / 34.1 (32k); code prefill 427.8 / 350.2; free at load 497 MiB. It printed
no op table: decode runs on CUDA graphs, and the timer needs graphs off. "P36 rerun as p36b: the timer needs
GGML_CUDA_DISABLE_GRAPHS=1."

P36b (graphs off): decode 15.2 (4k) / 14.7 (32k) under the timer; code prefill 429.1 / 351.0; free at load 511.
"Per-node events without graphs inflate small ops (decode 15 tok/s under the timer) - the table ranks, it does not
measure."

Mat-vec microbench (test-backend-ops perf, n=1; no receipt file, numbers from the notebook):

| shape | time | bandwidth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 17408x5120 | 54.5 us | 357 GB/s (80 % of 448) |
| 5120x17408 | 56.9 us | - |
| 10240x5120 | 34.9 us | - |
| 6144x5120 | 23.7 us | 290 GB/s |

"Summed over one token's mat-vecs ~16.8 ms vs 22.9 ms measured raw at 4k: ~6 ms is the ~900 other kernels (MUL x258,
RMS_NORM x129, SCALE x96, CONT/CPY/SSM_CONV/L2_NORM/SIGMOID x48 each, GDN x48). Ceiling arithmetic: all weights
(~5.6 GB/token incl. the 248k-row output head) at 448 GB/s = 80 tok/s; mat-vecs at 80% + zero overhead = 64 raw. 60
raw is out of reach at stock memory; 60 with drafting (x1.18) needs the target step at ~19.5 ms (-15%): small-op
fusion is the lever."

The rig log's last decode table (32k, timer on, 62.7 ms per graph) shows the PTQ1_0 mat-vecs at cols=1
(e.g. `MUL_MAT ptq1_0 5120x17408 cols=1` x88), which P55b later cited.

## Deviations, incidents, void runs

P36 (first run) is void for the op table (graphs on). P36b is the valid run.

## Verdict

Headline: "decode time is 74% PTQ1_0 mat-vec at 65-80% of DRAM peak; ~6 ms/token is small ops". By the gate's
branches: mat-vecs are > 60 % of the step but above 50 % of peak, so the Turing mat-vec is not the lever; the
remaining ~6 ms of small ops is. Arithmetic note: 16.8 / 22.9 = 73.4 %; the notebook writes 74 %.

## Corrections later

- P37c measured the split directly with skip arms: 15.8 ms mat-vec + 2.8 ms small ops + ~3.5 ms rest per token. The
  small-op share is smaller than the ~6 ms estimated here.
- P38: memory +1000 MHz gave +7.7 % plain decode, which confirms the step is largely bandwidth-bound.
- 2026-10-09 decision: stop micro-fusions (each class 0.5-1.7 %, P76 gate+up +0.2 %).

## What it changed

Moved the effort from draft count to the target step; started P37 (skip test) and P38 (memory clock).

## Toolbox (for the next model)

- Method: an op timer with CUDA graphs off ranks ops but inflates small kernels (decode fell from 43.3 to 15.2
  tok/s under the timer). Use it for ranking only; use microbenches and skip tests for milliseconds.
- Model-specific: 5.6 GB of weights per token incl. a 248k-row output head; mat-vec shapes 17408x5120 etc.
- Transfers: compute the bandwidth ceiling first (weights per token / peak GB/s). At 80 % of peak on the mat-vecs,
  kernel work on them has little room; the rest of the step is overhead to fuse or to remove.
