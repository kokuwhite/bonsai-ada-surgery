## Question

Does our 8 GB configuration beat sudoingX's published 8 GB recipe on this card, at every depth he serves?

## Why

~23:50 note (Cary, going to bed): "make our build better and faster than sudoingX's - treat his as the floor." His 8 GB
recipe (bonsai2-small-gpu serve/8gb.sh): -c 65536, q4_0 K/V, no MTP head, all in VRAM, branch sudoingX/llama.cpp
bonsai2 (ff41412). "His mmvq.cu:299 enables the PT path for PTQ1_0 on cc >= TURING for every column count, so on this
card his build already has what #325 gives the Prism fork: #325 is parity with his branch on Turing, not a lead over it."

## Plan and gate

~23:50 plan: "build his branch for sm_75 (vendor/sudo-bonsai2, bin-sudo), run his exact recipe, and our best 8 GB
configuration, same probe, decode at 4k / 16k / 32k / 60k, stock clocks + fan 100%, alternating. Floor gate: ours must
beat his at every depth he can serve (his window ends at 64k) - and the same KL/identity honesty applies."
2026-10-07 plan: "Arms, alternated, depths 4k/16k/32k/60k: sudo1 (his serve/8gb.sh verbatim: -c 65536 q4_0 all-VRAM,
no head) / ours-C2 (q4 head, pool, N=38400, MAX_KB=1024) / ours-B (no head, N=77568, MAX_KB=1024) / sudo2.
Gate (Cary's floor): our default beats sudo at every depth he serves, by more than sudo1-vs-sudo2 spread; else name the
depths where it does not and why."

## Method

run_arm.sh per arm: stop, idle check, slot 5 (175 W), serve, quick_tps.py at 4000, 16000, 32000, 60000 (3 prompts x
400 tokens; "Say hi." before each depth; the first prompt's prefill is the incremental part of the filler), acceptance
from the log, free VRAM at the end. Order: sudo1 00:41:29, oursC2 00:48:50, oursB 00:54:58, sudo2 01:04:00.

## Deviations, incidents, void runs

- C2 at 32k and 60k generated one token on all three prompts (server log: normal releases, no error). P012's C2 (same
  N, PCT=0) produced 400 tokens at 32k. The only configuration difference noted at the time: GGML_CUDA_L2_PREFETCH_MAX_KB
  =1024 (prefetch on). "Prefetch code cannot change values (prefetch.global.L2 only) - P17 (2x2 drafting x prefetch,
  text captured) decides before anything else uses prefetch."
- The sudo build ran concurrently with P014b (CPU load during P014b arms).

## Verdict

"sudoingX's floor on this card; ours wins decode, loses prefill; C2 broke at depth (open)." "Floor status: NOT yet
beaten on prefill; decode beaten."

## Corrections later

- P017: prefetch cleared; texts identical with prefetch on/off. The 32k request had restored a 16k prefix from the RAM
  prompt cache ("P13 log: 16,524 of 32,040 tokens evaluated") and its first sampled token was end-of-turn.
- P018 reproduced it (tail 2 only, after a prompt-cache restore); P054 (2026-10-09) attributed it most likely to the
  shared CUDA pool race (fixed by patch 0045).
- P014c: without the prefill cliff our no-head prefill is within 3-6% of sudo's, but that comparison used a different
  request sequence (see P014 corrections); P023: on the same sequence our incremental prefill at depth is -15% (32k) /
  -21% (60k).
- P025-P027: f16 prefill from pool memory + ub 1024 brought long-mode prefill above the floor at 4k-32k and to a tie at
  60k.

## What it changed

Set the floor numbers used for the rest of the mission: decode 37.2 / 30.4 / 24.5 / 17.5 and prefill 427 / 382 / 320 /
256 at 4k / 16k / 32k / 60k (docs/8GB.md, and the 2026-10-11 card TODO). Opened the prefill work (P014-P016, P023,
P025-P027) and the one-token bug work (P017, P018, P021, P029).

## Toolbox

- Method: build the strongest public competitor recipe on the same card, run it verbatim through the same probe, and
  alternate it before and after your own arms (sudo1/sudo2) to get its spread.
- Finding: an all-in-VRAM 64k window with f16-converted FA prefill (his fork) is a strong prefill baseline on Turing;
  decode wins come from the PT kernel (both), the MTP head, and more positions in VRAM.
- Lesson: count one-token answers in every depth sweep; a 0.0 tok/s mean is a correctness failure, not a slow run.
- Model-specific: none beyond Bonsai's PTQ1_0 support in both forks.
